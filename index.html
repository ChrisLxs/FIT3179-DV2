Understanding Healthcare Accessibility in Malaysia

Healthcare resources are not distributed equally across Malaysia. Differences in population size, geographical location, and healthcare infrastructure can influence the availability of medical services.

This dashboard explores three key questions:

  1. How does hospital bed availability differ across Malaysian states?
  2. Where are public healthcare facilities located, and how are they distributed relative to population?
  3. How have healthcare resources changed over time, and what differences exist between states?

Data includes public healthcare facility records, hospital bed statistics, and population estimates. Resource availability is used as an indicator, not a direct measurement of healthcare quality or travel-time accessibility.

Hospital Beds per 10,000 Residents (2024)

Comparing hospital bed capacity relative to population across Malaysian states and federal territories.

Key Finding: Large Differences in Bed Availability

Putrajaya has approximately 70.16 hospital beds per 10,000 residents, compared with only 11.32 in Selangor. Kuala Lumpur records 26.59 beds per 10,000 residents.

This suggests substantial differences in hospital bed availability relative to resident population. However, hospitals may serve patients from neighbouring states, so these figures should not be interpreted as direct measures of healthcare accessibility.

Note: Higher bed availability does not necessarily indicate better healthcare access. Hospitals may also serve patients from neighbouring states.

Hospital Bed Availability Over Time (2015–2024)

Explore how hospital bed capacity per 10,000 residents has changed over time. Select a state or federal territory to examine its trend.

Key Finding: Healthcare Capacity Changes Over Time

Hospital bed availability can change over time as both the number of hospital beds and the resident population change.

Use the state selector to compare 2015 and 2024. An increasing ratio indicates that hospital beds have grown faster than population, while a decreasing ratio indicates that population has grown faster than hospital bed capacity.

These trends help identify states where healthcare capacity may not be keeping pace with population growth.

Changes in the ratio may reflect changes in hospital bed capacity, population size, or both.

Public Healthcare Facilities per 100,000 Residents

Compare the number of recorded open public healthcare facilities relative to each state's 2024 population.

Key Finding: Selangor Has the Lowest Facility-to-Population Ratio

Selangor records approximately 5.15 public healthcare facilities per 100,000 residents, compared with 31.33 in Perlis and 31.30 in Pahang.

This difference highlights how facility counts relative to population vary substantially between Malaysian states.

However, a lower ratio does not necessarily mean poorer healthcare access. Individual facilities differ in capacity, services, and patient coverage. Residents may also use facilities outside their home state.

Facility counts are based on the available facility registry and divided by 2024 population estimates. Facilities may differ substantially in size and services.

Healthcare Facilities vs Hospital Bed Availability

Explore the relationship between public healthcare facilities per 100,000 residents and hospital beds per 10,000 residents across Malaysian states.

Key Finding: More Facilities Do Not Always Mean More Hospital Beds

Kuala Lumpur records approximately 6.92 public healthcare facilities per 100,000 residents, but has 26.59 hospital beds per 10,000 residents. In comparison, Perlis has 31.33 facilities per 100,000 residents but only 17.42 hospital beds per 10,000 residents.

This demonstrates that facility density and hospital bed availability measure different aspects of healthcare resources. A state may have relatively few facilities but substantial hospital bed capacity.

Use the dropdown to exclude Putrajaya, whose unusually high bed-to-population ratio can make differences between other states harder to see. The comparison is descriptive and does not establish healthcare accessibility or a causal relationship.

Facility counts are based on the available registry, while hospital beds and population are from 2024. These measures describe resource availability, not actual healthcare accessibility or quality.
